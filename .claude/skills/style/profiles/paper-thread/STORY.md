# Paper Thread: story, rhythm, audio

Read after `STYLE.md`. This file describes how a video in this profile is told. The structure below is a framework to fill with new content, not a script to translate.

## 1. Storytelling framework

### The shape

A video in this profile answers one question: **"Why is this one thing remarkable?"** It opens on the result, then walks back through what made it hard, then widens to why it matters, and ends on the result again.

| Beat | Share of runtime | Job | In the reference (for orientation only) |
|---|---|---|---|
| A. Result first | 0 to 4 % | State what happened in one plain sentence over the strongest real image. One-word headline. | 0 to 3 s |
| B. Contrast | 4 to 7 % | One sentence that says what normally happens instead. A tally makes it concrete. | 3 to 7 s |
| C. Promise | 7 to 12 % | "Give me a minute and I'll show you why..." Name the payoff and its size. A journey move starts here. | 7 to 11 s |
| D. Scale | 12 to 30 % | 2 to 4 stations that make the subject's size, speed, distance or count physical through comparison with something familiar. | 11 to 27 s |
| E. Known vs new | 30 to 50 % | Acknowledge what the audience thinks they already know, then draw the line between that and this. End on a short flat sentence. | 27 to 47 s |
| F. Mechanism under constraint | 50 to 78 % | How it actually works, told as a sequence of problems: it cannot do X, so it does Y. Live numbers run. Includes a "it has no A, no B, no C" stack. Ends on the risk. | 47 to 76 s |
| G. Why | 78 to 88 % | Ask the obvious question out loud as a headline, answer it in a chain of short causes. | 76 to 84 s |
| H. Widen and return | 88 to 100 % | One sentence of history or big picture, then the result sentence again, cut so the last words lead into the first words. | 84 to 94 s |

Shares are guides. Beats D to F can be reordered or resized to fit the topic; A, C, G and H should always exist.

### Hook rules

- First sentence = subject, verb, object, under 12 words, no setup. The surprising result itself, not a question about it.
- Second sentence = the contrast that makes the first one surprising.
- Third sentence = explicit promise with a time cost ("a minute") and a superlative that the video then earns.
- The first frame already has the thread, a headline word and a dated plate label. Nothing fades in.

### Voice of the script

- Short declarative sentences, average about 14 words, many under 8. Present tense for mechanism, past for the event.
- Concrete nouns and numbers over adjectives. Every claim of size gets a number and a comparison.
- Devices that recur: the rule of three ("no A, no B, and no C"), cause chains ("because A is B, and B is C"), the corrected assumption ("X does this all the time. But X is only half of it."), the flat stop ("Nobody had ever done it."), the asked-aloud question ("Why risk that?").
- No greetings, no "in this video", no call to action in the voice. No jokes; dryness is the tone. Mild understatement is fine.
- One idea per sentence so that each sentence can own one visual change.

### Attention mechanics

- An open loop from the promise that closes only in beat G.
- A new visual fact at least every second (see rhythm below), but only one thing to read at a time.
- Escalation by scale: each station in beat D should be bigger or stranger than the last.
- Tension by subtraction in beat F: remove the tools the subject would normally have, one by one.
- The loop ending: the final image is the first image, reached by a camera move into the thread's head, and the final words are grammatically completed by the first words.

## 2. Editing and rhythm

Reference measurements: 94 s, 30 fps, about 17 stations, about 296 spoken words.

- Station length: 2 to 14 s, median about 5 s. Short stations (2 to 3 s) for single facts, long ones (8 to 14 s) when the same picture keeps gaining information.
- Inside a station something changes every 0.5 to 1.0 s: a word of a headline, a counter step, a label, a line being drawn, a plate arriving. These are additions to a still picture, never a re-layout.
- Between stations: one camera move of 0.3 to 0.5 s. The move starts on the last word of the outgoing sentence or the first word of the incoming one, never mid-phrase.
- Roughly 85 % of the runtime is held picture with small additions; roughly 15 % is travel. If more than a quarter of the video is camera movement, it has lost the style.
- Plates: 4 to 7 per 90 s, each on screen 2 to 5 s, each tied to the exact sentence it proves.
- Headlines: 5 to 8 per 90 s. Counters: 6 to 10. Do not show a headline and a counter at once unless one is finished.
- The opening and closing live shots last about 3 s each.
- Breathing points: after each flat-stop sentence leave the picture untouched for 0.3 to 0.5 s before the camera moves.

## 3. Audio direction

What could be measured: voice-over runs wall to wall; pauses between sentences are 0.15 to 0.55 s and none is longer; integrated loudness about -20 LUFS in the recording; a continuous low bed sits roughly 12 to 15 dB under the voice. Character of the music and exact sound effects could not be identified reliably from the recording, so the guidance on them below is a recommendation, not a measurement.

### Voice-over

- One narrator, calm, low-key, fast: about 185 words per minute in English in the reference. For Czech aim for 140 to 160 words per minute and cut the script rather than speed up the voice.
- Even delivery with small downward stops on the flat sentences. No hype, no rising "influencer" intonation.
- Record or generate the final voice first; every visual timing comes from its word timestamps.

### Music (recommendation)

- A steady, low, unobtrusive bed: sustained pad or pulse, no melody that competes with speech, no drops, no build that ends before the video does. Constant level from first frame to last so the loop is seamless.
- Mix: voice clearly on top; bed 12 to 15 dB below it.

### Sound effects (recommendation)

- Sparse and physical, matched to paper and ink: a soft tick for counter steps, a pen scratch for line draw-ons, a paper slide for plates, a short low whoosh for camera travel, one firm stamp for a headline word.
- Keep them under the voice. No risers, no impacts, no glitch sounds.
